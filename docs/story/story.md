# Main Story Draft

Character profiles: [Vael](../characters/vael.md), [Ilyr Voss](../characters/ilyr-voss.md), [The Assassin](../characters/assassin.md), and [The Barbarian](../characters/barbarian.md).

## Premise

A dark elf rogue wakes alone on a desolate beach with no memory of his name, his past, or how he arrived.

He retains instincts for survival and combat, but every personal detail is gone. He has never been to this island before.

## Opening Quest: Ashore Without a Name

The first arc begins with the hero scavenging the shoreline and following signs of recent conflict inland.

Early goals:

- Survive the coast and reach the nearest settlement.
- Learn basic movement, combat, and traversal through environmental danger.
- Recover the first memory fragments tied to a shattered voyage.

Narrative tone:

- Isolation, disorientation, and mistrust.
- Fragmented clues that suggest betrayal rather than accident.

## Hidden Truth (Unknown To The Player)

Before the game begins, the rogue was traveling by ship to the island with a wizard and the wizard's associates: an assassin and a barbarian. The assassin helped conceal the expedition's purpose but grows afraid of what the wizard intends to become. The barbarian remains loyal to the wizard and hunts the rogue after learning he survived.

The wizard's true objective is to find the Book of All, an all-powerful tome that holds knowledge of everything, including magic.

During that voyage, the wizard double-crossed him. The wizard struck him with a powerful spell, sending him overboard into the storm-dark sea. Believing him dead, the wizard and associates continued on to the island.

The hero survived, drifted to shore, and woke with his memory erased.

## Story Function Of Amnesia

The memory loss is not only backstory; it is a progression mechanic.

- New regions and relic encounters trigger memory fragments.
- Recovered memories recontextualize allies, enemies, and motives.
- The hero's identity unfolds in parallel with gameplay unlocks.

## Early Narrative Questions

- Who is the wizard, and why was the rogue targeted?
- What was the group's true objective on the island?
- Was the memory loss caused only by the spell, or by something older on the island?
- What did the rogue know before the betrayal?

## Draft Throughline

From amnesiac survivor to hunted witness to deliberate avenger:

1. Survive and orient.
2. Discover the betrayal.
3. Recover identity.
4. Confront the wizard and the larger conspiracy behind the island expedition.

## Chapter Outline

### Chapter 1: Waking Coast

Vael wakes in the black surf with no name and only his instincts, then follows shattered oath-stones and signs of a recent landing toward Tidefen Village. The hostile coast teaches him to survive while its memory-stripped carvings respond faintly to his touch, suggesting a connection he cannot explain. Tidefen's salvagers tell him that a young wizard named Ilyr has been buying charts to the inner island, and a memory-knot ritual restores a flash of Vael standing on a storm-tossed ship as someone behind him begins to cast a spell. Realizing his arrival was no accident, Vael follows Ilyr's trail into the jungle.

### Chapter 2: Low Canopy Jungle

Vael crosses the Low Canopy's ruined medicine terraces, where altered predators guard reliefs of wardens carrying the Book of All inland, and descends through Smuggler Inlet to the Pirate Fort. There he discovers that Ilyr purchased a route to the island's deep vaults by granting the corsairs arcane protection. The barbarian from Vael's fractured memories attacks on Ilyr's orders and calls Vael a dead man who should have stayed beneath the waves, but Vael survives and forces him to retreat. A memory echo from Ilyr's abandoned charts restores the expedition's true objective: they came to claim the Book, whose knowledge could unmake every limit placed upon magic.

### Chapter 3: Underroot

Seeking a way past the island's sealed heights, Vael earns passage through divided Emberroot Village and descends into the memory-resonant Underroot caves, where old warding script repeats moments from his lost life. The assassin shadows him through the caverns and covertly disables a trap, claiming only that Ilyr must not reach the vault yet. In the Relic Chamber, Vael rejects a vision of effortless power and awakens the double-jump sigil, which recognizes his blood as oathbound to the island's seals. The relic restores the full betrayal aboard the ship: Ilyr struck Vael from behind and cast him into the sea because only Vael could open the final vault, and Ilyr believed a dead key was safer than an unwilling one.

### Chapter 4: High Canopy

Using the relic to reopen elevated routes, Vael climbs through Canopy Shaft into the suspended ruins of the High Canopy and pursues the assassin along abandoned courier paths toward Observatory Ridge. Cornered beneath the celestial instruments, she admits that she helped Ilyr hide the expedition's purpose and stood aside during the betrayal, but now believes the Book will turn his desire for order into tyranny. Vael accepts her route through the observatory without granting forgiveness, and together they align its instruments to expose a path across the northern island. The activation also broadcasts Vael's oathbound signature, revealing his survival to Ilyr and transforming the expedition from a secret race into an open hunt.

### Chapter 5: Northern Reaches

Vael crosses the storm-beaten North Shoals and reactivates the broken spans of the Floating Causeway while Ilyr's scouts and the barbarian close in on him. At Glassmire Village, mirrored wetlands and layered oral histories resist the island's memory erosion, allowing the elders to compare Vael's recovered fragments with records they have guarded for generations. They reveal that Vael once trained in the rites of the wardens and joined Ilyr's expedition willingly to prevent the Book from being misused, unaware that Ilyr had recruited him chiefly because his oathbound blood could open the Book Vault. When the barbarian assaults Glassmire rather than accept that truth, the assassin openly turns against Ilyr and commits herself to stopping him, though neither she nor Vael pretends that her choice erases what she did.

### Chapter 6: Stormwatch Tower

Vael and the assassin assault Stormwatch Tower before Ilyr can finish projecting lock-breaking frequencies toward Blackreef, fighting upward through weather engines and unstable ritual chambers while the barbarian defends his master's work. Vael defeats the barbarian but refuses to kill him, leaving him with evidence that Ilyr always intended to sacrifice every member of the expedition once the vault opened. At the tower's summit, Vael claims the arcane tuning key and confronts Ilyr through a ritual projection. Ilyr argues that complete knowledge is the only cure for chaos and reveals the last part of Vael's choice to join the voyage: Vael had hoped to judge the Book for himself, making his present pursuit more than revenge and forcing him to question whether his original certainty was another form of ambition.

### Chapter 7: Blackreef Approach

With the arcane key, Vael passes through Ironwake Village and rallies a limited defense before crossing the exhausted battlements of the Castle Approach. Ilyr drives pirates, mercenaries, and arcane constructs against the prison-temple, sacrificing his own forces to strip away its outer seals, while Ironwake prepares to burn rather than let the breach spread west. The barbarian returns wounded and finally turns on Ilyr after seeing the promised new order begin with the disposal of its most loyal servant. He holds the collapsing approach against Ilyr's remaining forces so Vael and the assassin can enter Blackreef, a costly act that rejects his master without excusing the people he harmed in that master's name.

### Chapter 8: Blackreef Castle

Vael and the assassin cross Blackreef Castle's doctrine halls and weaponized memory rites, where each chamber tests whether their motives differ from Ilyr's hunger for control, and descend to the Book Vault as Ilyr breaks its final outer seal. Vael's oathbound blood opens the inner lock, but the vault magnifies both men's deepest justifications: Ilyr sees perfect order achieved through absolute knowledge, while Vael confronts his desire to make betrayal and uncertainty impossible. After defeating Ilyr, Vael reaches the Book of All with his identity restored but no command from his former self. The final decision belongs to the person he has become: destroy or reseal the Book, bind it to himself at personal cost, or divide its knowledge into guarded fragments so no single will can possess it all.
